Pest control license requirements by state

Short answer

In all 12 states we checked, you need a business license for each location and a certified person who qualifies it by passing the state’s exams. Most also require 1 to 3 years of experience (3 in Florida, 2 in California, Georgia and North Carolina). Most listed business license fees run $35 to $300.

What it takes to license a pest control company in 12 big pest-control states: the business license, the certified person who has to qualify it, the experience they need, insurance minimums and the state’s own fees. Every row links to the official source.

How pest control licensing works

Almost every state licenses two things separately. The business gets a license, registration or charter for each location. A certified person (called a certified operator, qualifying manager, qualifying party or responsible certified applicator, depending on the state) has to be attached to that business and passes the exams. Technicians who do the spraying usually register under that person.

That’s why the experience rule matters most. You can form the company on day one, but in most states it can’t work until someone with enough documented field time has passed the exams and is named on the license.

The federal baseline sits underneath: under EPA’s certification rule, anyone who uses or supervises restricted-use pesticides must be a certified applicator under a state plan (40 CFR Part 171). The states below go further and cover general-use structural work too.

Requirements in 12 states

Structural (household and commercial building) pest control, summarized from each agency’s own pages and rules. Jump to: Texas · Florida · California · Georgia · North Carolina · Arizona · Ohio · Pennsylvania · New York · Illinois · Virginia · Tennessee

Texas

Texas Department of Agriculture, Structural Pest Control Service (SPCS)

Business license: Commercial business license for each business location, including branch offices. You can’t advertise or solicit until it’s issued.

Who qualifies it: A responsible commercial certified applicator registered with the business, who can’t be the responsible applicator for any other business or location.

Experience: 12 months of verifiable pest control experience in the past 24 months plus at least 6 months holding an SPCS technician license; or a qualifying biological-science degree; or 12 of the past 24 months of related industry experience. Technicians first complete 20 classroom hours, 40 on-the-job and 8 classroom hours per category, and a 6-hour approved course.

Insurance: At least $500,000 bodily injury and property damage per occurrence, $1,000,000 aggregate, on TDA form ALS-1101.

Listed state fees: Commercial business license (per location): $300; Certified applicator license: $125. Each apprentice or technician also registers for $125. Exam fees are paid to TDA’s testing vendor (Metro) after TDA pre-approves you.

Official sources: TDA: business license · TDA: exams and eligibility · TDA: technician licensing

Florida

Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS)

Business license: A business license for each pest control business location, renewed every year.

Who qualifies it: A full-time certified operator in charge, certified in every category the location works in (general household pest and rodent, termite and other WDO, lawn and ornamental, fumigation).

Experience: Three years as a service employee of a licensee in the category, one of them in Florida immediately before you apply; or a qualifying degree (20 semester hours in entomology, botany, agronomy or horticulture) plus one year. FDACS’s exam application also asks you to document at least 45 jobs per category.

Insurance: $250,000 per person and $500,000 per occurrence bodily injury, plus $250,000 per occurrence and $500,000 aggregate property damage, or a $500,000 combined single limit.

Listed state fees: Business license (per location, yearly): $300; Certified operator exam (per category): $300. Employee ID cards are $10 each, and the operator certificate renews at $150 a year.

Official sources: FDACS: licensing and certification · FDACS: fee schedule · F.S. 482.132

California

Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB)

Business license: Company registration with the SPCB, in each branch you work in (Branch 1 fumigation, Branch 2 general pest, Branch 3 wood-destroying organisms).

Who qualifies it: A Qualifying Manager: a licensed Operator in the same branch who supervises the company’s daily business.

Experience: For a Branch 2 Operator license: two years of Branch 2 field experience, including one year as a licensed Branch 2 Field Representative (one year = 1,600 hours of field work), plus board-approved pre-operator courses taken within 3 years before the exam. Live Scan fingerprints.

Insurance: $500,000 general liability, a $12,500 surety bond, and workers’ compensation (or a filed exemption).

Listed state fees: Operator exam: $100; Operator license: $150. The company registration fee comes in the SPCB registration packet.

Official sources: SPCB: become an Operator · SPCB: start a company · SPCB: examinations

Georgia

Georgia Department of Agriculture, Structural Pest Control

Business license: A structural pest control company license for anyone doing the work for hire.

Who qualifies it: A Designated Certified Operator (DCO) for each category. The owner can be the DCO if certified; otherwise hire one.

Experience: Two years of verifiable structural pest control experience (one within the last five years), or a degree in entomology, public health or a related subject plus one year of service experience in the last five. Pass the core exam plus a category exam. No reciprocity with other states.

Insurance: Household pest and fumigation: $50,000 bodily injury and $50,000 property damage per occurrence, $200,000 annual aggregate. Wood-destroying organisms: $100,000 / $100,000 / $500,000 plus errors and omissions. Pollution liability for sudden and accidental release.

Listed state fees: Certified operator exam (per category): $45; Certified operator credential: $100. The company license fee and a required UGA urban entomology research fee are listed on the application.

Official sources: GDA: company license · GDA: certified operator license · GDA: insurance form (PDF)

North Carolina

NCDA&CS Structural Pest Control and Pesticides Division

Business license: A structural pest control license (held by a person, by phase: P household pests, W wood-destroying organisms, F fumigation) to work on property you or your employer don’t own.

Who qualifies it: The licensee must first be an NC Certified Applicator in good standing in that phase.

Experience: Two years of independently verifiable experience in that phase (employer affidavits, or customer letters if self-employed), then pass the phase exam. FBI background check dated within 6 months. College training can substitute for some experience.

Insurance: Proof of insurance, sent by the insurer to NCDA&CS, before the license is issued. Amounts are set in the rules.

Listed state fees: License, first phase (yearly): $200; License exam (per attempt, per phase): $50; Certified applicator ID card: $50. Each extra phase adds $75; employee ID cards are $40 each.

Official sources: NCDA&CS: structural licensing · NCDA&CS: license exams · G.S. 106-65.31 (fees)

Arizona

Arizona Department of Agriculture, Pest Management Division (PMD)

Business license: A PMD business license for anyone who advertises, inspects, bids on or applies pesticides for structural pest management.

Who qualifies it: A Qualifying Party (QP): a Qualified Applicator registered as responsible for training, equipping and supervising the company’s applicators.

Experience: To be a Qualified Applicator: 24 months as a certified applicator in the category within the last 10 years, or 12 months plus qualifying coursework or a degree, or 24 months of pest management business experience in a state that didn’t require a license. Pass core and category exams at 75%. Fingerprint background check.

Insurance: Proof of financial security filed with the business license application.

Listed state fees: Business license: $185; Qualified Applicator application: $75; QP registration (filed with the business license): $0. Certified Applicators (your techs) apply for $55 each.

Official sources: AZDA PMD: FAQs (requirements and fees) · A.A.C. R3-8-103 (fees)

Ohio

Ohio Department of Agriculture

Business license: A pesticide business license, with each registered location listed, to apply pesticides for hire. The licensing period runs October 1 to September 30.

Who qualifies it: Licensed commercial applicators do the work.

Experience: A commercial applicator license needs a passing score on the exams for each pesticide use category you apply in.

Insurance: Proof of financial responsibility, required before the license is issued and each renewal.

Listed state fees: Pesticide business license (and each added location): $35; Commercial applicator license: $35. Late renewals cost 50% more.

Official sources: OAC 901:5-11-03 (business license) · OAC 901:5-11-04 (commercial applicator)

Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture

Business license: A pesticide application business license, renewed every year.

Who qualifies it: At least one certified applicator. Technicians who apply under supervision register with the department.

Experience: Commercial applicators pass the core exam and a category exam.

Insurance: At least $100,000 bodily injury and $100,000 property damage per occurrence, covering completed operations and pesticide application, with a deductible no higher than $2,500.

Listed state fees: Business license (yearly): $35; Commercial applicator certificate (yearly): $40; Core exam: $50; Category exam: $10. Commercial technician registration is $30 a year.

Official sources: PDA: pesticide application businesses · 7 Pa. Code §128.3 (fees) · 7 Pa. Code §128.34 (insurance)

New York

NYS Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC)

Business license: A business registration for each business address. It lasts about 3 years, with the expiry date set by DEC region.

Who qualifies it: A certified applicator in each category. For the structural subcategories (7a–7d, 7f, 7g) a technician isn’t enough.

Experience: One year as a certified technician plus 12 hours of category training, or two years as a technician; or three years of full-time apprentice experience within the last five years plus the core and category exams.

Insurance: Commercial general liability of at least $1,000,000 per occurrence, with NYSDEC named as certificate holder.

Listed state fees: Business registration (about 3 years): $900; Applicator certification, first category: $450; Exams: core + one category ($100 each): $200. Each extra category or subcategory is $150.

Official sources: NYSDEC: business registration · 6 NYCRR 325.8 (eligibility) · ECL §33-0911 (fees)

Illinois

Illinois Department of Public Health (Structural Pest Control)

Business license: A commercial structural pest control business license for each business location, renewed by December 1 every year.

Who qualifies it: At least one certified technician at the business.

Experience: General-use pesticides: pass the General Standards exam. Restricted-use pesticides: also pass subcategory exams. Certification renews every 3 years.

Insurance: A certificate of insurance on IDPH’s own form, sent by your insurer.

Listed state fees: Business license (per location): $250. Technician exam fees are on IDPH’s technician application.

Official sources: IDPH: structural pest control · IDPH: business license application (PDF)

Virginia

Virginia Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (VDACS)

Business license: A pesticide business license for each location or outlet. It expires March 31 every year.

Who qualifies it: A certified commercial applicator. The applicant or an employee also passes the business license exam, unless they’re already certified.

Experience: One year as a Virginia Registered Technician, or one year of pesticide-related education, training or experience. Certificates expire June 30 of the second year after issue.

Insurance: At least $100,000 property damage, $100,000 for injury or death of one person, and $300,000 per occurrence.

Listed state fees: Business license (per location, yearly): $75; Commercial applicator certificate: $25. Reexamination is $25.

Official sources: VDACS: pesticide business license · 2VAC5-675-50 (business fee) · 2VAC5-675-30 (applicator fee)

Tennessee

Tennessee Department of Agriculture

Business license: A pesticide charter for each office that does commercial pest control. Charters expire June 30 of odd-numbered years.

Who qualifies it: Each charter names the licensed pest control operator(s) who supervise the work, plus a roster of registered solicitors and technicians.

Experience: The supervising operator must hold a Tennessee license in the categories offered. See the department’s certification page for the exam steps.

Insurance: Proof of a bond and liability insurance. If either lapses the charter expires at once, with a 30-day grace period to send new proof.

Listed state fees: Not stated as a flat amount (see note). The charter fee and the per-person technician fee are tiered fees under T.C.A. §43-1-703 and can be prorated in the first term; the current amounts are on the charter application.

Official sources: Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0080-09-04-.03 (charters) · TDA: certification, licensing and charters

FAQ

Do I need a license to start a pest control business?

In every state we checked, yes. Each one requires a business license, registration or charter for the company, and a certified or licensed person who qualifies it. Who that person must be, and how much experience they need, is what varies most.

Can I get a pest control license with no experience?

Usually not to qualify a business on your own. Most of the 12 states require one to three years of documented experience for the person in charge (for example 2 years in California, Georgia and North Carolina, 3 years in Florida), though a related degree can shorten it in several states. A common path is to work as a registered technician first, or to hire a certified operator to qualify your company while you get your own hours.

How much does a pest control license cost?

The state fees themselves are modest. In the table, the business license runs from $35 a year (Ohio, Pennsylvania) to $300 (Texas, Florida) or $900 for a roughly 3-year New York registration, and exams run from $10 to $300 each. Insurance, bonds, training courses and your time cost far more. All figures are listed state fees checked October 17, 2026.

Is a pest control license good in other states?

Generally no. Georgia and North Carolina both say they have no reciprocal licensing agreements, and most states make you meet their own exam and experience rules. Arizona lets the director waive exams in whole or in part for someone certified in another state.
