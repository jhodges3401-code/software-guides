The federal baseline for restricted-use pesticides

Under EPA’s certification rule, state plans must require certified commercial applicators to keep records of restricted-use pesticide (RUP) applications for at least 2 years. The record covers: customer name and address; location; crop or site; date and time; product name and EPA registration number; amount; and the certified applicator’s name and certification number. Supervised applications also name the noncertified applicator. Source: 40 CFR 171.303. Most state rules above cover every application, not just RUPs.

FAQ

How long do I have to keep pest control application records?

In the 12 states we checked it’s 2 or 3 years. Texas, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Virginia and Tennessee require 2 years; North Carolina requires 2 years beyond the last treatment; California, Arizona, Ohio, Pennsylvania and New York require 3. Some record types run longer, such as Florida’s preconstruction termite records (3 years). Check your state’s rule, linked in the table.

Is there a federal requirement?

Yes, for restricted-use pesticides (RUPs). EPA’s certification rule requires state plans to make certified commercial applicators keep RUP application records for at least 2 years (40 CFR 171.303). Most state rules go further and cover general-use pesticides too.

Can I keep records electronically?

Most rules care about the content and about handing records over on request, not the format. Florida’s rule, for example, says electronic records must be provided to the department on request. Confirm with your state agency.

Is this legal advice?

No. It’s a plain-English summary of official rules, checked October 17, 2026, with a link to each source. Rules change, and the official text is what counts. If you’re unsure, call your state’s lead agency.
